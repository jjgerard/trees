About the tree studio

A free drawing tool for syntax trees that are ready to publish, for people who already know how trees work.

What it is for

Making the tree figures that go into papers, handouts, slides and theses, without compiling anything to see what you got. You can build a tree by dragging pieces, or paste one in as labelled brackets or LaTeX and have it drawn, and then adjust it by hand until it looks the way you want. It does not check your analysis: nothing stops you joining any piece to any other.

What it can do

  • Build by dragging. XP, X′, X⁰, triangle phrases, words, labels and a row of category chips (X, C, T, v, V, D, N, P, A, Adv, or any you type). Pieces join when dropped just under another, pull off when dragged away, and a category chip dropped on a node renames it.
  • Paste or copy text. Labelled brackets, LaTeX qtree, forest and TikZ, with the text following whatever you do on the drawing.
  • Movement arrows drawn by dragging from the trace to where it moved, plus triangles, crossed-out text, traces, and several trees on one page.
  • Typography. Font, size, bold, italic and colour, separately for labels and for words, with overrides for any single node. Labels take subscripts, superscripts, primes, italics and Greek (NP_i, T^0, V', \phi).
  • Keyboard shortcuts for building and editing without the mouse. Press ? in the studio for the list.

Getting trees out

  • SVG, PNG and PDF, drawn to real point sizes, so 12 pt text is 12 pt in the file. PNG can be 150, 300 or 600 dpi.
  • TikZ, as text or a standalone .tex file, with every position, triangle and arrow written out exactly.
  • forest carries your arrows as \draw lines; qtree cannot carry arrows.
  • A saved file (.json) that keeps positions, arrows and styles so you can reopen a tree later.
SVG text is saved as text in the fonts you picked, so it only looks right on a computer that has those fonts. PNG and PDF are made on your own computer and are safe to send to anyone.

Your work stays with you

Trees are saved in your own browser so a refresh does not lose them. Nothing is uploaded: there is no account and no server that receives your trees. Clearing your browser’s site data removes the saved tree, so use Export → Save this tree as a file for anything you want to keep.

Open source

The studio is a single static page with no build step and no dependencies, released under the MIT licence. The source is at github.com/jjgerard/trees. You can download the repository and open index.html to run it offline, or host it anywhere.

Credits

The drag-to-build approach is in the spirit of TreeForm, and the export formats are meant to slot into the LaTeX tools syntacticians already use (qtree, forest, TikZ).
