How the tree studio works

Or paste a tree. Open { } LaTeX / text, then paste a labelled bracketing ([S [NP …] [VP …]]) or LaTeX (\Tree [.S …], qtree or forest) in there. The tree is drawn as you type. The box also follows anything you do on the drawing, in the format you pick, ready to copy into a paper.

Build. Drag pieces from Parts onto the page (XP, X′, X⁰, a triangle, words, labels, and a category chip to rename a node). Drop a piece just under another to hang it there, or just above a top node to become its parent; drag a node away to pull it off; drag a piece onto the Parts shelf to put it away. In Nudge mode (top of the page) dragging only slides a node on the page, to fine-tune spacing; hold Shift to slide just that node.

Edit by hand. Click a node to select it. Double-click to retype it. The keyboard does most of it: arrows walk the tree, Tab / F1 adds a daughter, Shift+Tab / F2 a mother, Enter a sister, Delete removes it. Press ? for the full list. Pieces you moved keep their place when you edit the text.

Triangles collapse a phrase to its words. Movement arrows: select the trace, then drag the small green dot beside it onto the node it moved to. (Or press ↷ Arrow on the bar next to the node and click the target.) Drag the round handle on an arrow to change how far it swings.

Type. Fonts, sizes, bold/italic and colour are separate for labels and for words, and any node can override them. Labels take _i, ^0, V' → V′, \emph{…}, \sout{…} and Greek like \phi.

View. Drag the background or scroll to pan; Ctrl/⌘ + scroll or pinch to zoom; Fit brings everything back.

Out. Export gives SVG, PNG, or PDF (via print). The text box gives brackets, qtree and forest. Positions you dragged and arrows are in the pictures but can’t be written in qtree or forest, which place everything themselves.

Glossed examples (interlinear text) are on the Glosses tab at the top.

How the gloss page works

Type it. Each example has three boxes: the example, its word-by-word gloss, and the free translation. Separate words with spaces. Word 1 of the example sits over item 1 of the gloss, and so on, so the columns always line up. If the two lines have a different number of words the empty place is marked in red.

Inside a word. Hyphens, dots, equals signs and so on are just text. To make one gloss item that contains a space, put it in braces: {the dog}. CAPITALS in the gloss (3SG, PST) are set in small caps and written as \textsc{3sg}. \emph{…}, \textbf{…} and \textsc{…} work anywhere.

Several examples. + Add example adds another. Under Numbering, choose one number each, or one number with a. b. c. for a set.

LaTeX. Press { } LaTeX to show the box, pick gb4e, linguex, expex or plain tabular, and copy the result into your paper. You can also paste your own \gll (gb4e, linguex) or \gla … \glb … (expex) into the box and the page reads it. gb4e, linguex and expex number their examples themselves, so “No numbers” only applies to the picture and the plain tabular.

📷 Photo reads glossed examples from a photo or a page of a book. Check every word: handwriting, special characters and the word-by-word pairing can all be misread.
